### Defra UK-AIR and AURN

UK-AIR provides an **Annual and Exceedance Statistics** tool where you can select networks, pollutants, monitoring sites, metrics and years. It includes annual means and exceedances of UK Air Quality Strategy objectives and European limit values. However, I found no option that automatically classifies each AURN station as above or below the WHO 2021 guidelines. 

UK-AIR does define and calculate a **running annual mean**, meaning a value calculated from the preceding year of measurements. However, its published use appears mainly connected with particular statutory objectives, such as 1,3-butadiene, rather than a public rolling WHO dashboard for PM2.5, PM10 or NO2. 

Therefore, UK-AIR supplies most of the underlying measurements needed, but someone still has to calculate and present the WHO status.

### Northern Ireland is the closest official example

The annual **Air Pollution in Northern Ireland** report explicitly compares individual monitoring sites with the WHO 2021 guidelines.

For 2024, for example, it reported:

- two sites above the WHO PM10 annual guideline
- all but one assessed site above the WHO PM2.5 annual guideline
- 14 of 16 sites above the WHO NO2 annual guideline

It also assesses WHO short-term guidelines using annual percentile statistics. This is a very close match to your proposed **calendar-year** calculations, but it is published as an annual report rather than a continuously updated station dashboard. 

### Breathe London also publishes useful WHO comparisons

Breathe London sensor pages refer directly to **WHO annual guidelines** and **WHO daily guidelines**. Its annual network reports classify site-level calendar-year averages against WHO values, normally subject to a minimum data-capture requirement. 

Its 2021 and 2022 report included individual site annual averages and WHO-based banding. It reported that all sites with sufficient data exceeded the WHO annual guidelines for NO2 and PM2.5 in those years. However, Breathe London nodes are indicative sensor instruments, rather than equivalent to AURN reference analysers, and I did not find a network-wide rolling 365-day WHO status product. 

### A local rolling-year precedent exists

Air Quality England previously hosted monthly Hounslow reports containing **rolling annual means** for NO2, PM10 and PM2.5, calculated over the preceding 12 months. That proves the presentation model has been used in the UK, but these were borough-wide summaries, provisional and not explicitly classified against WHO 2021 guidelines. 

### Other sources do not quite meet the requirement

The London Air Quality Network currently offers a WHO display based on the older **2005 WHO guidelines** and says that implementation of the updated 2021 standards is forthcoming. It therefore is not currently the station-level WHO 2021 annual product you are describing. 

The WHO Ambient Air Quality Database provides annual concentrations for towns and cities, but it is designed to represent city-level exposure rather than individual monitoring stations, and is updated periodically rather than providing live rolling years. 

## What this means for UK AQ

There appears to be a genuine gap that your WHO feature could fill, particularly by combining AURN with other UK networks.

I would present two clearly separated results:

1. **Calendar year**
   - one result per sensor and pollutant
   - completed calendar years
   - data-capture percentage
   - provisional or ratified status
   - above, below or insufficient data

2. **Rolling 365 days**
   - updated daily
   - preceding 365-day mean
   - above, below or insufficient data
   - clearly labelled as a rolling comparison with the WHO annual guideline

I would use wording such as **“above the WHO 2021 guideline”** rather than **“non-compliant”**. WHO guidelines are health-based recommendations, not UK statutory compliance limits, and the rolling 365-day calculation would be your own useful application of the WHO annual concentration rather than a separately defined WHO compliance period. 

So, **Northern Ireland and Breathe London offer calendar-year precedents, and Hounslow offers a rolling-year precedent, but I found nowhere that currently combines both across AURN and wider UK sensors.**

For example, its reporting has stated how many monitoring sites met the WHO PM10 annual guideline in a particular year. ### 3. GOV.UK AURN data contains everything needed, but does not do the WHO comparison

The official GOV.UK service provides AURN hourly measurements, daily averages and annual averages. Its archive also provides annual means and exceedance statistics. However, the comparisons are principally against:

- UK Air Quality Objectives
- UK legal limit values
- Environment Act targets
- Historically, relevant European limit values

The official PM2.5 reporting, for example, assesses stations against England’s 10 µg/m³ annual mean concentration target, rather than the WHO guideline of 5 µg/m³. 

The new GOV.UK local air quality dashboard is also not the answer. It excludes national-network measurements such as AURN, relies on local-authority annual reports and assesses results against national Air Quality Objectives rather than WHO guidelines. 

### 4. Air Quality England

- Monthly statistics
- Running means
- Exceedance statistics
- Historic downloads

However, its stated assessment framework is based mainly on UK objectives and legislation. It does not appear to provide a standard nationwide WHO annual or trailing-365-day pass/fail flag. Some individual local-authority reports hosted there manually include WHO comparisons, but they are fragmented rather than part of a unified service. 

## What the calculation would need to show

For the WHO 2021 annual guidelines:

| Pollutant | WHO annual guideline |
|---|---:|
| PM2.5 | 5 µg/m³ |
| PM10 | 15 µg/m³ |
| NO₂ | 10 µg/m³ |

For the WHO daily guidelines:

| Pollutant | WHO daily guideline |
|---|---:|
| PM2.5 | 15 µg/m³ |
| PM10 | 45 µg/m³ |
| NO₂ | 25 µg/m³ |
| O₃ | 100 µg/m³, based on the daily maximum 8-hour mean |

The WHO short-term guidelines are expressed using the 99th percentile, broadly allowing three or four days above the guideline during a year, rather than requiring every single day to be below it. 

For your purposes, each AURN station could therefore have:

- **Calendar year:** mean from 1 January to 31 December.
- **Rolling year:** mean for the latest 365-day period, recalculated daily.
- **WHO annual result:** above or below the annual guideline.
- **WHO daily result:** number of valid days above the daily guideline, plus the 99th-percentile daily value.
- **Data coverage:** a visible completeness percentage, with no classification when coverage is insufficient.
- **Provisional/final status:** current rolling periods would generally use provisional data, while older calendar years could use ratified AURN data.

The annual guideline must be compared with the **average over the whole applicable period**, not with individual hourly readings.

## Bottom line

**Breathe London is the closest existing live presentation, and LAQN reports provide some calendar-year WHO comparisons. For nationwide AURN stations, there does not appear to be an existing publisher offering both calendar-year and rolling-365-day WHO status.**

The official AURN data needed to build it is available, so this could be a distinctive feature of UK AQ: a nationwide station-by-station WHO dashboard showing completed calendar years alongside a live trailing-year assessment.
