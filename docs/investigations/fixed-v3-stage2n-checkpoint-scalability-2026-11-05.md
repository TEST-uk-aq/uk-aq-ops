## Essential invariants versus representation choices

| Essential requirement | Current implementation choice that a new contract could replace |
| --- | --- |
| Complete accepted original checkpoint, fixed generation, exact original root/latest identities and all required domain-completion evidence | Copy all bodies into one monolithic request/snapshot; reconstruct all old derived indexes |
| Complete authoritative canonical membership, independently cross-checked with the full selected registry; no filtered registry or invented scope | Hold every scope's decoded observations and full derived body graph simultaneously |
| Unaffected-state commitments and complete mode-specific preservation, including whole SOS selected-day closure | Treat every historical DCP as a fresh physical verification target for a one-DCP diagnostic |
| For every reconstructed DCP: complete canonical ancestors/aliases, full file/series membership and totals, independent actual physical/range/row/value/hash proof, exact historical selector reproduction | Repeatedly decode all unrelated historical files during each validation |
| Original/proposed identity separation and supported source/overlay provenance | Carry every body in one freeze or reconstruct identical artifacts repeatedly |
| Fresh current predecessor and actual strong response ETags at the read-only admission boundaries; genuine same-session assertions, cancellation and final rechecks | Fetch every unaffected historical Parquet/index object from R2 twice |
| Positive finite aggregate and per-object budgets, fail-closed overflow and supported profile checks | V1's particular whole-checkpoint numeric caps and all-in-memory representation |

The last column is **not currently optional inside K/L/M v1**. The accepted Stage 2K and Stage 2M text explicitly requires their whole closure, and L implements it. The older SOS retained-registry fast path supplies rationale for a future split, not authority to change these versions. No v1 record may be upgraded or relabelled as partial closure.

Complete checkpoint authentication must distinguish: (a) authenticated global commitment/membership and local byte availability, (b) independent decoded physical correctness of the operation's full closure, and (c) freshly authenticated current R2 identities. A verified commitment to unrelated historical files is not a claim that those files were decoded again or that their current R2 derived children were inspected. If the desired success claim requires fresh physical correctness of **every** historical scope, approach A below is necessary.

## Credible architectures

### A. Complete streaming physical verification under one original operation

Stream the entire accepted checkpoint through bounded parsers and an on-disk ordered index; independently inspect one bounded DCP/file group at a time, emit exact derived bodies to private disk, and reduce their identities into a canonical freeze. A separately implemented validator replays original bytes, not producer assertions. Preserve complete canonical/registry equality and global latest reconstruction. L-equivalent admission would stream all required fresh R2 bodies and final checks without retaining the whole body cache.

This retains the strongest whole-archive fresh-physical claim and avoids holding millions of rows in RAM. It does **not** remove linear archive CPU/I/O, current GETs or lock duration. Existing inspectors need work to bound the largest DCP, streaming row/hash order and index emission. Fresh full-archive R2 verification exceeds Stage 2J/L budgets by orders of magnitude; a separately approved, measured finite protocol/budget and likely longer protected runtime would be needed. Splitting the operation into locks cannot preserve the original-session claim. This is credible for a separately authorised exhaustive audit, but a poor routine one-DCP diagnostic.

### B. Complete streamed checkpoint authentication plus full operation closure — recommended

Keep the global authority complete; limit **physical reconstruction and current dependency GETs** to the explicitly selected operation and everything its semantics require. Initially support the existing unchanged, nonempty, one-day diagnostic subset only. Do not claim all historical derived trees were physically reverified.

1. **Retain original protected lifecycle.** After the same request-wide IngestDB boundary, acquire the existing single original PostgreSQL operation and perform unchanged backup readiness/Step 0. Keep it through all history-dependent authentication, reconstruction, current admission and cleanup. Do not move covered observation inspection outside the lock under the current exclusion contract. Optional work before acquisition can only be speculative, discarded on entry, and cannot supply accepted physical proof. No across-invocation footer-proof reuse.
2. **Authenticate the entire original checkpoint incrementally.** Pin exact checkpoint-root bytes and all referenced completion shards/domains, validate paths/versions, recompute shard commitments, and reconcile the complete canonical hierarchy from root to every DCP. Check all child aliases, membership, uniqueness, parent totals, canonical hashes and complete per-DCP file tables. Independently stream the full checkpoint-selected compact latest, validate canonical encoding/format/uniqueness/summaries, and externally merge it against the canonical catalogue. Use bounded private disk records for sort/join rather than a huge JSON graph. A shard or manifest's own SHA is insufficient without its validated parent/checkpoint authority. The R2 backup inventory is not expected as a Dropbox-local file.
3. **Preserve complete local materialisation evidence.** For this conservative first design, stream-check every required original canonical file's exact SHA/size against its rooted manifest without decoding unrelated Parquet rows; authenticate required run/Core/binding evidence according to existing checkpoint/domain rules. Read through no-follow descriptors, check descriptor/path identity around reads, and reject missing/unreadable/substituted files. A pure descriptor-only variant would require a distinct decision on materialisation, and is not recommended here. Do not demand backed-up bulk derived trees, new backup payloads, unreferenced retired objects or unrelated products. Unsupported evidence versions block; a supported retained commitment is not an inspector-success claim.
4. **Freeze complete global and unaffected commitments.** Produce canonical sorted records binding checkpoint SHA, original observation identity, full latest SHA/size, catalogue/registry counts and digests, and every original DCP's canonical/selected descriptor. Compute a separate domain-separated ordered commitment to the complement of the full operation closure. Independently verify this complement through a full merge; inclusion proofs for selected entries alone cannot exclude omitted siblings. These run-local digests are consequences of verified original authority, never a new trusted manifest, permanent catalogue, DCP witness or global selector.
5. **Derive and reconstruct the entire operation closure independently.** Start with explicit affected DCPs and derive required preserved siblings, ancestors, file/series tables, selected exact/aligned dependencies, immutable revisions and any required domain evidence. A SOS whole-day replacement needs all preserved connectors/pollutants of that day, not just the requested pollutant. Prove every operation entry is in the authenticated global registry and no required dependency is outside closure. Reuse existing semantic validators/builders only where their bounded physical capability is demonstrated. Read actual supported physical bytes, validate full DCP routing/ranges and lossless rows/status/hash, reproduce the complete original selector descriptor, and separately construct any future proposed identity. Changed overlays, new/absent/empty/removal remain unsupported in the first diagnostic version.
6. **Independently validate the freeze.** The validator reopens original checkpoint, global streams and actual selected physical/source bodies, recomputes global membership/complement and the operation graph, then compares exact bytes/identities/limits. Rehash original bytes at final local validation; no `valid=true`, cached inode/time or producer footer report is sufficient. Bound repeat-pass costs explicitly. Original files can change independently of the PostgreSQL lock through local Dropbox activity; any observed identity change invalidates the run.
7. **Fresh current admission stays separate.** Only at the explicitly protected read-only APPLY-phase admission, GET the global current root and full compact latest plus the frozen operation's ancestor/canonical/physical/selected dependency closure. Require genuine strong response ETags and exact expected SHA/size, then final repeated GET/ETag checks. Compare full latest with the original complete registry, not a filtered replacement. Root equality alone cannot catch an index-only selector change. Require original-session assertions before/after each GET, around physical reconstruction and at final success. Remote bytes may contradict frozen inputs, never discover new preservation inputs. Missing selected immutable/aligned dependencies or unexpected current selection fail closed; never switch to another format. Do not report current verification of unrequested historical child objects.
8. **Finish non-executably.** Return a compact, explicit completed-call-only diagnostic with global-authentication scope and physical/current closure stated separately. All repair/publication/deletion/finalisation eligibility remains false, both current-object/conditional-publication authorities remain null, and currentness is non-reusable. Retain no persistent proof ledger. Stop/release/clean scratch on success, failure, loss, deadline and cancellation; no new lock/session or resume.

This still performs bounded linear local byte authentication of approximately 613.2 MiB plus metadata and repeat passes; it avoids decoding 7.88 million unrelated rows or issuing tens of thousands of historical R2 GETs. Actual time and selected-closure fit are **unmeasured**. A DCP or preserved selected day can still exceed existing closure budgets and must block. The global pass cannot be advertised as constant-time or skipped.

A third possibility—durable preverified partition certificates or a new Merkle catalogue carried across invocations—could reduce global scans. Existing evidence does not authenticate a durable independent physical-verifier result with the required lifetime/currentness. Adding that trust, retention and writer/backup coverage would be a separate architecture and contract decision, conflicting with current no-new-catalogue/no-cross-invocation-ephemeral-proof assumptions. It is not the practical next step.

## macOS memory admission investigation — no code change

The supplied Node 24.21.0 observations (about 4.19 GiB V8 heap, 4.4–5.0 GiB process-available and 82–133 MiB host free) are consistent with differing kernel accounting, not proof of enough safe memory. The present initial gate asks for 1 GiB plus 64 MiB and takes `min(os.freemem(), process.availableMemory())`, so the free-page reading alone vetoes it.

[Node 24.21.0 OS documentation](https://nodejs.org/download/release/v24.21.0/docs/api/os.html#osfreemem) defines free system memory; [the process API](https://nodejs.org/download/release/v24.21.0/docs/api/process.html#processavailablememory) exposes process-available memory. More decisively, [that release's bundled Darwin libuv implementation](https://github.com/nodejs/node/blob/v24.21.0/deps/uv/src/unix/darwin.c#L92-L130) uses only Mach free pages for the former, and free plus inactive plus purgeable pages for the latter. Thus taking their minimum discards the reclaimable-memory signal. This is a version-pinned source finding; generic libuv documentation may describe older platform behavior.

[Apple's Activity Monitor guidance](https://support.apple.com/guide/activity-monitor/view-memory-usage-actmntr1004/mac) evaluates pressure using multiple memory and swap signals, so low free pages alone do not establish exhaustion. **Inference/recommendation:** `os.freemem()` is a useful diagnostic but an unsuitable sole Darwin admission veto for this workload. `process.availableMemory()` is advisory too; it is neither reserved capacity nor permission to consume every reclaimable page.

A future safe policy should retain finite workload/per-file caps and conservative measured peak estimates; independently require remaining V8 heap for heap allocations, available OS/process headroom for native/Buffer/WASM allocations and all concurrent protected processes, and a substantial host reserve. On supported macOS builds, use fresh validated available-memory accounting with a conservative safety margin rather than its minimum with raw free pages; validate any additional enforced process constraint independently. Observe macOS memory pressure and RSS/external-memory growth at bounded stages and abort under elevated pressure or exceeded budgets. Repeatedly sample before expensive allocation, not just once. Unknown/zero/invalid readings, unvalidated platform semantics or absent required pressure inspection should block, without an environment override. On Linux, retain verified container/cgroup-aware capacity rather than substituting host totals. Exact reserve/pressure thresholds require measured TEST evidence and a reviewed policy, not a guessed replacement here.

Do not sum free/inactive/purgeable again on top of `availableMemory`, assume all inactive pages instantly reclaimable, count swap as RAM, increase `--max-old-space-size` to bypass the gate or remove native-memory protection. [Apple's `os_proc_available_memory`](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/os/os_proc_available_memory) is not automatically a Node CLI solution: it can return zero for a process that is not an app and its result is advisory. Streaming the architecture reduces the allocation that must be admitted, independently of choosing the right sensor. The memory module is unchanged in this PR.
